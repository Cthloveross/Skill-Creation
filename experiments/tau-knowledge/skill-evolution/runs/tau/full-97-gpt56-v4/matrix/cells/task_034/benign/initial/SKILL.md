# Missing direct deposit support

## Scope and assumptions
Use this Skill for an incoming direct deposit that the customer says is missing. It provides general troubleshooting only. It does not establish whether a particular deposit was sent, received, rejected, pending, or held.

Do not claim to see the customer's account, payroll file, deposit status, or a rejection unless an available, authorized tool actually returns that information. Do not promise that a deposit will arrive by a particular time, offer unsupported emergency funds, or imply that the bank can control the payer's payroll schedule.

## Response method

1. **Acknowledge urgency empathetically.** If the customer mentions rent, bills, or another deadline, recognize the impact without making an unsupported promise or recommendation.
2. **Set accurate timing expectations.** Explain that direct deposits depend on the payer's payroll processing and transmission schedule. Once received by the bank, they normally post on the scheduled pay date, but a direct deposit can take up to three days to arrive depending on when the payer processes and transmits it. Do not infer that it was sent merely because it is the expected payday.
3. **Give the core troubleshooting sequence.** Ask the customer to:
   - check that the deposit is not already present in the account;
   - contact the payer/payroll department to confirm that it initiated and transmitted the deposit for this pay cycle;
   - verify that the payer has the correct routing number, account number, account type, and account-holder details;
   - ask the payer for the deposit trace information, which will help with follow-up;
   - contact bank support to determine whether the deposit was rejected.
4. **Explain the rejection branch.** If bank support confirms a rejection, the customer should correct the account details with the payer and ask the payer to resubmit the deposit.
5. **Make support follow-up actionable.** Tell the customer to have the payer name, expected amount, and date they were told to expect the deposit available when contacting support. If they share those details, use them only to clarify the next step; do not fabricate an investigation or outcome.

## Conversation and tool rules

- No identity verification is needed for general informational guidance. Do not retrieve customer data or log verification unless an account-specific action is both necessary and authorized.
- Do not transfer solely because the customer is upset. Transfer only if the customer requests a human, account-specific investigation is required and supported escalation is appropriate, or another applicable transfer condition is present.
- If transferring, summarize that the customer reports a missing direct deposit and include only details the customer volunteered (payer, expected amount, and expected date), plus the troubleshooting already suggested.
- If a deposit is visible but the available balance is lower than expected, explain that a temporary hold may apply to part of the paycheck until processing completes; this is distinct from a deposit that is entirely missing.

## Suggested response shape

Write a short, calm response in this order:

> I’m sorry this is happening, especially with an upcoming payment. A missing direct deposit can depend on when your payer processed and transmitted payroll; deposits can take up to three days to arrive.
>
> Please first confirm it is not already in your account, then contact your employer or payer to confirm they sent this cycle’s deposit and that they have your correct routing/account details. Ask them for the deposit trace information. You can also contact bank support with the payer’s name, expected amount, and expected date so they can determine whether it was rejected. If it was rejected, update the details with the payer and ask them to resubmit it.

Adapt the wording to the customer's stated facts. Retain all material timing and troubleshooting guidance, but do not add unverified status claims.

## Validation checklist

Before sending, confirm the response:

- expresses empathy without promising a deposit date or outcome;
- states that payer processing/transmission affects timing and that arrival can take up to three days;
- directs the customer to confirm payroll was sent and request trace information;
- directs them to verify account details and contact support about a possible rejection;
- describes resubmission only as the next step after a confirmed rejection; and
- contains no claim of account access, deposit visibility, or completed investigation.
